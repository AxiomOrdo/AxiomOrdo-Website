The engagement

The client problem

Inspection pressure exposes uncertainty, not just missing paperwork.

A vessel may have procedures, records and corrective actions in place while the shore team still lacks a reliable view of what can be produced, explained and demonstrated when asked.

The review creates a usable readiness position.

It brings the agreed evidence into one structured review, tests where the record is incomplete or unclear, and turns the result into owned next actions.

Built for a specific decision

What should this operator confirm, correct or escalate before the expected scrutiny? The engagement is scoped around that question—not a generic “AI compliance” exercise.

Bounded scope

What we can review

The final scope depends on vessel type, operating context, inspection horizon and the evidence you choose to provide. A typical review can cover:

01

Certificates and controlled documents

Availability, currency and internal consistency of the selected certificates, endorsements, manuals, plans and SMS-controlled documents.

02

Operational readiness evidence

Selected records supporting drills, maintenance, life-saving and fire-safety arrangements, pollution prevention, navigation and crew welfare.

03

SMS implementation

How the selected procedures are reflected in records, responsibilities, familiarisation, reporting and practical onboard use.

04

Findings and corrective action

Recent PSC, class, flag, internal-audit or inspection items supplied by the client, including the evidence supporting closure.

05

Vessel and shore alignment

Whether the reviewed information presents a consistent position between onboard personnel and the shore-side team.

06

Focused priority area

A defined concern such as recurring deficiencies, document control, emergency preparedness or readiness for a known inspection window.

Concrete outputs

What you receive

The review concludes with a bounded management position, not an unsupported compliance score or a promise about inspection outcomes.

Scope and evidence record

A clear record of the vessel, topics and documents reviewed, along with material requested but not made available.

Readiness findings register

Observed gaps, inconsistencies and points requiring confirmation, each linked to the evidence reviewed rather than assumption.

Prioritised action plan

Recommended actions ordered by readiness importance, with proposed ownership and the evidence needed to demonstrate completion.

Management summary

A concise view of the main readiness themes, immediate decisions and issues that need escalation or competent specialist input.

Review debrief

A working session with the nominated client team to explain the findings, challenge ambiguity and agree practical next steps.

Source and limitation notes

Visible boundaries around the materials used, items not assessed and conclusions that depend on further verification.

Who it is for

For teams responsible for the real inspection position.

The service is intended for vessel operators and managers, DPAs, QHSE and marine assurance teams, superintendents, masters and officers who need a structured independent readiness view.

What this review is not

This is an independent advisory readiness and compliance review. It is not a statutory survey, flag-state inspection, class audit, certification service or legal opinion. Meriden does not issue or renew certificates, determine regulatory compliance on behalf of an authority, or guarantee a PSC outcome. The operator, master and relevant competent authorities retain their own responsibilities and decisions.
