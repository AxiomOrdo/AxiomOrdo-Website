Who it is for

This checklist works at both ends of the gangway: for vessel personnel checking readiness directly, and for shore-side teams trying to surface the real inspection picture early.

Masters & officersUse it as a practical walk-round and record check before inspection.
DPAsUse it as a fast reality check on what the vessel can actually evidence.
QHSE managersUse it to structure preparation, close gaps, and improve inspection discipline.
Smaller operatorsUseful where there is no large shore-based compliance department behind the vessel.

What it covers

Seven core readiness areas plus a final ten-point walk-round focused on the things people miss.

Certificates & documentation

Statutory certificates, endorsements, safe manning alignment, officer documentation, and on-board SMS records.

Life-saving appliances

Lifeboats, liferafts, HRUs, pyrotechnics, muster lists, and drill evidence.

Fire safety

Fire doors, dampers, alarms, fixed systems, extinguishers, firefighter equipment, and escape routes.

Pollution prevention

Oil Record Book, OWS, garbage records, sewage arrangements, fuel records, and ballast-water consistency.

MLC & the human element

Hours of rest, SEAs, medicals, wages, welfare, and complaint procedures.

Navigation & ISM

Charts, bridge equipment, passage plans, GMDSS, SMS accessibility, maintenance evidence, and NCR close-out.

Why this matters

PSC problems rarely start with one dramatic failure. They usually start in the smaller gaps: expired drill evidence, blocked routes, inconsistent records, weak document control, or a crew that cannot quickly find the right procedure.

The point of this checklist is simple: find those gaps before someone external does.

If you are crew and this helps, send it to your DPA or QHSE lead.
Good readiness often starts with someone on board who cared enough to pass the problem up before inspection day.
